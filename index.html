Which Tables Are Included
in Table 10?

Table 10 includes four drilling classes — Table D, Table E, Table F and Table H. They are the lettered flange tables of BS 10, and Indian and Gulf piping trades still order by the letter rather than by a bar or PN figure. The letter is not a material grade and not a finish: it is a drilling and thickness pattern. Move up the letters and the flange gets a wider rim, a bigger bolt circle, more or larger bolts and more meat through the hub.

Each table fixes five things for every nominal bore — outside diameter (OD), pitch circle diameter (PCD), number of bolt holes, bolt hole diameter and flange thickness. Two flanges bolt together only when all four of the first values agree. A Table E and a Table F flange of the same bore will not always mate, and that is the single most common reason a consignment gets rejected on site.

How do you tell which table an existing flange is? Measure the outside diameter across the rim, measure the pitch circle centre-to-centre through two opposite holes, count the holes and check a hole diameter, then read those four numbers off the chart for that bore. If you would rather not measure, send a photo with a tape laid across the face on WhatsApp and we will identify it.

Table D – the lightest of the four

Nominally in the region of 50 psi working pressure. Low-head water, tank nozzles, vents and gravity lines. Thinnest section, so it is the cheapest per piece where the duty allows it.

Table E – the everyday choice

Around 100 psi. This is the table most Indian municipal water, sewage and fire-fighting mains are built to, which is why it is the grid published in full alongside.

Table F – the step up for pumps

Around 150 psi. Specified on pumping mains, compressed air headers and process lines where surge or shut-head pressure rules out Table E.

Table H – the heaviest in common use

Around 250 psi. Boiler feed, steam, high-head pumping and hydraulic duty. Thickest section and the largest bolting of the four, so allow for the extra plate weight when budgeting.

What are Table 10 flanges?

+

Table 10 flanges are mild steel pipe flanges drilled to the lettered tables of the BS 10 flange series, the table system still written into most Indian and Gulf piping specifications. Rather than a class number, the drilling is called out by a letter such as Table D, Table E, Table F or Table H. Each letter fixes the flange outside diameter, bolt pitch circle diameter, bolt hole count, bolt hole size and flange thickness for every nominal bore. I.S. Engineering Co. machines the Table 10 range from 15mm NB to 600mm NB at its Darukhana, Mumbai works.

Which tables are included in Table 10?

+

The four Table 10 classes Indian and Gulf projects order in volume are Table D, Table E, Table F and Table H. They step up in duty: Table D is the lightest at roughly 50 psi working pressure, Table E takes about 100 psi, Table F about 150 psi and Table H about 250 psi. For the same nominal bore, a heavier table means a thicker flange and heavier drilling. Confirm the table letter written on your line list or valve data sheet before ordering, because a flange from one table will not always bolt to another.

What is the difference between Table D, E, F and H flanges?

+

The difference is drilling and thickness, not bore. For a given nominal bore, Table D and Table E often carry the same outside diameter and bolt drilling with flange thickness being the main change, while Table F and Table H move to a larger bolt pitch circle and bigger or more numerous bolt holes as pressure duty rises. In practice Table D suits low-head water and vent duty, Table E is the default on municipal water, sewage and fire mains, Table F is specified on pumping mains, compressed air and process headers, and Table H is used on boiler feed, steam and high-head pumping service.

What sizes of Table 10 flanges does I.S. Engineering Co. make?

Table 10 flanges are made from 15mm NB up to 600mm NB. Regularly moving sizes are 15, 20, 25, 32, 40, 50, 65, 80, 100, 125, 150, 200, 250, 300 and 350mm NB, with 400, 450, 500 and 600mm NB cut to order. Anything outside that band, or any non-standard drilling, is machined against your drawing.

Can a Table E flange bolt to a Table F flange?

+

Only when the outside diameter, bolt pitch circle diameter, bolt hole count and bolt hole diameter of both flanges match. The Table 10 letters are drilled differently as duty rises, so two different tables in the same nominal bore will not always line up, and forcing a mismatched pair is how site teams end up with leaking joints and re-drilled flanges. Send both table letters and the nominal bore to I.S. Engineering Co. before you order and we will confirm whether the pair mates or whether an adaptor flange is needed.

How do I identify which table an existing flange belongs to?

+

Measure four things on the old flange: outside diameter, bolt pitch circle diameter, number of bolt holes and bolt hole diameter, then add flange thickness. Those five figures pin the flange to one table in the Table 10 series for that nominal bore. If you send the measurements, or a photo with a tape across the bolt circle, on WhatsApp to +91 99220 47887, our shop will identify the table and quote a matching replacement.
