How a Socket Weld Joint Is Actually Made

The flange bore is counterbored to a socket that matches the outside diameter of your pipe, the pipe is pushed in, pulled back about 1.6mm, and a fillet weld is run around the outside. That is the whole joint. No bevel, no root gap to hold, no backing purge on carbon steel work. A fitter with a mirror can make a sound socket weld on a 15mm branch in a place where a butt weld could never be inspected, which is exactly why small bore pipework is built this way.

The trade-off is the annulus. Between the pipe outside diameter and the socket wall there is a thin ring of dead space. On clean service — instrument air, steam tracing, hydraulic oil, dosing lines — that ring is harmless. On slurry, on anything that has to be pigged, and on service where crevice corrosion is a live concern, it is not, and the line should be built with weld neck flanges and butt welds instead. We would rather tell you that before you order than after.

Socket weld or weld neck — the short answer

Up to 80mm NB on clean service, socket weld is quicker, cheaper and easier to get right; above that, or on slurry, cyclic and critical duty, use weld neck. Most plants use both: weld neck on the header, socket weld on the small branches, tappings and instrument connections coming off it. We make both in the same shop, so a mixed list is one enquiry and one delivery.

SWRF — Socket Weld Raised Face

The one most people mean when they say socket weld flange. A raised sealing face puts the bolt load onto a narrow band of gasket, so it seals harder for the same torque. This is what small bore process lines, steam branches and hydraulic pipework are normally specified with. Facing, drilling and socket bore all to your drawing.

SWFF — Socket Weld Flat Face

The same flange with the face left flat, so the load spreads across the whole ring instead of a narrow band. Ask for this when the small bore connection lands on a cast iron pump casing, a cast valve body or a strainer — a raised face against cast iron is a cracked casting waiting to happen. Usually with a full face gasket.

RTJ / RJ Socket Weld

A ring groove is machined into the face and the seal is made by a metal octagonal or oval ring, not by a soft gasket. Specified on small bore high pressure and high temperature service, and on lines where a compressed fibre or spiral wound gasket will not survive. Tell us the ring number your drawing calls for.

Reducing Socket Weld Flange

Outside diameter and bolt pattern of the bigger size, socket bored for the smaller pipe. Useful when a 50mm bolt circle already exists on a vessel or a valve and you only need a 25mm line off it — one flange instead of a reducer plus a second joint. Send both numbers: the flange size and the pipe size.

Screwed / Threaded Flange (NPT or BSP)

Not a socket weld, but it does the same small bore job when a weld is out of the question — galvanised pipe you do not want to burn the coating off, a plant with a hot work permit problem, or a line that has to come apart again. Threaded to NPT or BSP as you specify. On hot or high pressure duty we will still suggest socket weld.

To Your Own Drawing

Odd socket depth, a non-standard bolt circle, an extra tapping in the hub, a thicker flange than the table gives, or a size nobody stocks. Send the drawing or a marked-up photograph of the flange you are matching and we will quote against it. This is a machining shop, not a catalogue.

Where Socket Weld Flanges Are Used

Socket weld flanges turn up wherever a line is small, the pressure is not, and there is no room to swing a torch for a butt weld. Hydraulic power packs, steam branches and tracing, instrument tappings, dosing skids, sampling points and the small utility runs that hang off every process header. Below are the duties our socket weld flanges most often go out on.

Hydraulic Power Packs

High pressure oil lines on presses, machine tools, hydraulic power packs and jacking systems are almost all small bore, and almost all socket welded. The joint is compact, it takes vibration well, and it can be made inside a crowded frame where a butt weld cannot.

Steam Branches & Tracing

Steam mains are large bore and butt welded; the branches, tracing lines and trap sets coming off them are 15mm to 40mm and socket welded. Raised face is the norm here, and the flange thickness on the table matters because these joints get hot, cool and get hot again.

Instrument Tappings & Air Headers

Pressure and flow tappings, gauge connections, instrument air headers and pneumatic control lines. Small, numerous, and usually the last thing to be routed — which is why they end up in awkward corners that only a socket weld or a screwed flange can serve.

Dosing, Sampling & Small Bore Process

Chemical dosing skids, reagent lines, sample points and analyser connections on process plant. Often in stainless SS 304 or SS 316 rather than mild steel, because the fluid is aggressive even though the flow is tiny. Tell us the grade with the size.

Pharma, Food & Clean Utility

Compressed air, purified water, CIP and clean utility branches in pharmaceutical and food plants, in SS 304L and SS 316L. Where the line has to be pigged or fully drainable, we will point you at weld neck instead — the socket annulus is not welcome on those duties.

Pipe Spool Fabrication Shops

Fabricators buy socket weld flanges by the bag because the joint is quick to fit and forgiving to weld — no bevel to grind, no root to chase, and the socket holds the flange square while the fitter tacks it. Repeat orders in Mumbai, Taloja and Bhiwandi run on this.

Socket Weld Flanges — Questions We Get Asked

What is a socket weld flange?

+

A socket weld flange has a counterbored socket in the hub. The pipe end drops into the socket and is closed with a single external fillet weld, so there is no root pass and no bevelling of the pipe end. That makes it the practical choice for small bore lines from 15mm to 80mm NB, especially in tight spaces where a butt weld cannot be made or inspected. I.S. Engineering Co. has been machining socket weld flanges at Darukhana, Mumbai since 1999, in mild steel IS:2062 and in stainless SS 304, 304L, 316 and 316L.

Above 80mm most engineers move to a weld neck or a slip-on flange, but if your drawing calls for a larger socket weld flange we will make it to the drawing. Stainless is asked for on chemical dosing lines, pharmaceutical and food process piping, instrument tappings on corrosive service and anywhere the line has to stay clean. What is the difference between SWRF and SWFF?

+

SWRF is a socket weld flange with a raised face and SWFF is the same flange with a flat face. The raised face concentrates the bolt load on a narrow band of gasket, which seals better and is what most process and steam lines specify. The flat face spreads the load across the whole face and is used when the flange bolts to a cast iron pump, valve or strainer body, because raised face loading can crack cast iron.

What is the difference between a socket weld flange and a weld neck flange?

+

A socket weld flange is fillet-welded on the outside of a socket; a weld neck flange is butt-welded to the pipe with a full penetration weld. Socket weld is quicker, needs no pipe bevelling and suits small bore up to 80mm NB. Weld neck is the stronger joint, it can be radiographed, and it is preferred on large bore, cyclic, high temperature and critical service. On the same plant you will often see weld neck on the header and socket weld on the small branches.

Why must the pipe be pulled back 1.6mm before welding?

+

Push the pipe fully home into the socket, then pull it back about 1.6mm (one sixteenth of an inch) before you tack it. That small gap gives the pipe end somewhere to expand into when the fillet weld heats it. Without the gap the trapped end can crack the socket bottom or the weld root. ASME B31.3 asks for this clearance on socket welded joints, and it costs nothing to get right at fit-up.

Which drilling table should I ask for?

+

Ask the drawing, not us — the drilling has to match whatever the flange bolts to. In Indian trade practice the BS 10 lettered tables are the usual reference: Table D for light duty, Table E for general service, Table F and Table H for higher pressure. We drill to Table D, E, F or H, or to an ANSI B16.5 class pattern if your line is specified in classes. If you are not sure which one you have, send a photo of the mating flange with its outside diameter and bolt hole spacing and we will identify it.

What is a reducing socket weld flange?

+

A reducing socket weld flange carries the outside diameter and bolt pattern of the larger size, but the socket is bored for the smaller pipe. It lets a small bore line land on an existing larger bolt circle without adding a separate reducer and one more joint. Give us both sizes — the flange size you have to bolt to, and the pipe size going into the socket.

Can I get a screwed flange instead of a socket weld flange?

+

Yes. A screwed or threaded flange takes the pipe on an NPT or BSP thread instead of a weld, and it does the same small bore job when welding is not allowed — galvanised pipe you do not want to burn, a site with a hot work restriction, or a temporary line. Tell us the thread form and size. If the service is high pressure or high temperature, a socket weld flange is still the better joint.
