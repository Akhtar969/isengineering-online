What Forging Changes, And What It Does Not

Forging changes the metal, not the dimensions. A forged 100mm NB Table E flange and a plate-cut 100mm NB Table E flange share the same outside diameter, the same pitch circle and the same holes — they bolt to the same mating flange and take the same gasket. What differs is what is going on inside the steel. In a forging the blank was squeezed into shape while hot, so the grain bends around the hub instead of being sliced straight through it, and porosity or segregation in the original stock is closed up and broken apart.

That matters most at the one place a flange is worked hardest — the fillet where the hub meets the back of the plate. That is where a flange under repeated pressure and temperature swings eventually cracks, and unbroken grain across that fillet is what buys the extra life. On a static, low-pressure water or air line it buys very little, which is why we will say so plainly when a plate-cut flange is the sensible thing to order.

What you are actually paying for in a forging:

  • Grain that follows the shape — unbroken metal flow around the hub and the fillet radius
  • A sounder blank — hot working closes porosity and disperses segregation in the stock
  • Better behaviour under cycling — pressure and temperature swings are what kill flanges, and this is where a forging earns its price
  • Toughness in the cold — relevant on refrigerated, cryogenic and exposed outdoor service
  • A grade the consultant will accept — most pressure specifications name a forging, and a plate-cut flange is simply rejected at inspection

When your specification insists on a forging

If the drawing or the specification page says ASTM A105, names a forging, or writes the line to ASME B31.1 or B31.3, then a forged flange is what the inspector will look for and there is nothing to negotiate. The same usually holds from Class 300 upwards. If the specification says nothing at all and the line is water, air, slurry or low-pressure service drilled to a BS 10 table, a plate-cut flange is ordinary Indian practice. Send us the specification page if you are unsure — it takes a minute to read and it can save you paying for a forging you did not need.

The Sub-Categories of Forged Flange

Forged is a description of how the blank was made, not a flange type — so a forged flange still has to be one of the five familiar patterns: weld neck, blind, slip-on, socket weld or threaded. All five are available as forgings from our Darukhana works, in mild steel and in stainless, and all five are machined here to the facing and the drilling you nominate. Pick the pattern by how the flange joins the pipe, then tell us it has to be a forging.

Forged Weld Neck (WNRF)

The pattern most pressure specifications name. The forged hub tapers down to the pipe wall thickness and is butt welded, so the joint is a full-penetration weld with no crevice and the stress spreads gradually out of the pipe into the flange. We bore it to the schedule of the pipe you are actually using — tell us Sch 40, Sch 80 or a wall thickness in millimetres.

Forged Blind (BLRF)

A solid forged disc, faced and drilled to match whatever it is bolting to, with no bore through it. Used to blank a vessel nozzle, close the end of a header, or cap a line that has been left for a future tie-in. Where you want a gauge, a drain or a vent through it, we tap the hole at the same setting.

Forged Slip-On (SORF)

A forged ring bored a little over the pipe outside diameter, so it slides on and is then welded on both the inside and the outside. Easier and quicker to line up on site than a weld neck, and perfectly adequate where the class allows it. Not the right answer on a line that cycles hard, because the two fillet welds carry everything.

Forged Socket Weld (SWRF)

For small bore. The pipe drops into a counterbore, seats on a shoulder, and is fillet welded on the outside only — there is nothing to weld on the inside, which is why it suits 15mm to 80mm NB instrument, sampling and utility tappings. Give us the pipe outside diameter and schedule so the socket is cut to fit that pipe.

Forged Threaded (NPT or BSP)

Tapped so the pipe screws straight in and no welding is needed anywhere. That is the point of it: on galvanised pipe a weld destroys the coating, and on a running plant a hot work permit may simply not be available. State NPT or BSP and the size, because the two thread forms are not interchangeable.

Where Forged Flanges Are Used

Forged flanges go on the lines where a failure is expensive — steam, gas, hydrocarbon, and anything written to a piping code. Below is the work our forged flanges actually leave Darukhana for.

Refinery & Petrochemical Lines

Process piping carrying hydrocarbon at temperature, where the specification names a forging and the inspector checks for it. Weld neck is the usual pattern; blinds go on the nozzles and the spare tie-in points.

Boiler & Steam Distribution

Steam headers, boiler connections and turbine-side piping. Steam lines heat up, cool down and do it again every start-up, and it is that cycling — not the peak pressure — that decides how long a flange lasts.

Gas Transmission & City Gas

Valve stations, meter skids, pig launchers and distribution headers. Gas work is inspected closely and the paperwork matters as much as the metal, so the test certificate goes out with the consignment.

Chemical & Pharmaceutical Plant

This is where the stainless goes — SS 304 / 304L for general duty, 316 / 316L where chlorides or acids are in the line. Socket weld and threaded forgings for the small-bore instrument tappings around the vessels.

Marine, Dockyard & Ship Repair

We are in Darukhana, so marine work walks in. Engine room and ballast line flanges, replacements taken off a worn part or measured on the ship, and one-off pieces wanted the same week rather than the next month.

Coded Fabrication & EPC Work

Fabricators and EPC contractors working to ASME B31.1 or B31.3, where the material grade, the marking and the certificate all have to line up with the purchase order before the spool is released.

Forged Flanges — Questions We Are Asked

What is a forged flange?

+

A forged flange starts life as a blank that was hammered or pressed into shape while the steel was hot, instead of being cut out of rolled plate. The forging is then turned, faced and drilled to size. Because the metal is worked hot, the grain follows the shape of the flange rather than running straight through it, and any porosity in the original stock is closed up — which is why pressure piping specifications usually call for a forged flange rather than a plate-cut one. I.S. Engineering Co. supplies forged flanges from Darukhana, Mumbai in mild steel IS:2062 and stainless SS 304 / 304L / 316 / 316L, 15mm to 600mm NB.

Which flange types do you supply as forgings?

+

Five — forged weld neck, forged blind, forged slip-on, forged socket weld and forged threaded. Each one starts as a forged blank and is then machined at our Mumbai works to the facing, the drilling and, on a weld neck, the bore you nominate. Anything outside those five, or any drilling that is not to a standard table, is made to your drawing.

Should I order forged or plate-cut flanges?

+

Order forged when the specification, the piping code or the pressure class asks for it, and plate-cut when it does not. On a low-pressure water, air or slurry line drilled to Table D or Table E, a plate-cut flange is ordinary Indian practice and there is nothing to gain by paying for a forging. Where the line carries steam, gas or hydrocarbon, or where the job is written to ASME B31.1 or B31.3, the specification will name a forging and that settles it. If you are not sure which side of that line your job falls on, send us the specification page and we will tell you what it is asking for.

Is IS:2062 the same as ASTM A105?

+

No. They are different specifications and they are not interchangeable. IS:2062 is an Indian structural steel and it is the grade our standard M.S. flanges are made from. ASTM A105 is an American specification written specifically for forged carbon steel piping components, with its own chemistry limits and its own testing. If your specification says A105, say so on the enquiry and we will work from A105 material and hand over the mill test certificate against it. Nobody should substitute one grade for the other on a pressure line without the consultant’s written approval.
