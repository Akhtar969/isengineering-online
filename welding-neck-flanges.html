How a Weld Neck Flange Differs From the Rest

The hub is the whole point. On a slip-on or socket weld flange the pipe sits inside the flange and is held by fillet welds, so the load changes direction abruptly at the weld toe. A weld neck flange instead tapers from full flange thickness down to the pipe wall thickness and is joined end-to-end with a butt weld, giving one continuous metal path from the bolt circle into the pipe.

Two practical consequences follow. The bore is machined to your pipe’s inside diameter, so there is no internal step for the flow to trip over — which matters on high-velocity, abrasive and slurry lines. And the butt weld is a full-penetration joint that can be radiographed or ultrasonically tested, which is usually what a third-party inspector wants to see.

Because the joint is end-to-end, the pipe wall thickness matters as much as the bore. Give us the schedule, or the actual wall in millimetres, and we cut the bore and the taper to suit it. Get that detail wrong at the ordering stage and your fitter ends up grinding a mismatch on site.

Order a weld neck flange with four numbers

  • Nominal bore — 15mm to 600mm NB
  • Drilling — Table D, E, F or H, or an ANSI class
  • Facing — RF, FF or RTJ
  • Pipe wall or schedule — so the bore and taper match

Weld Neck Flange Sub-Categories We Machine

Five weld neck sub-types are in regular production here: WNRF, LWN, RTJ (also written RJ), WNFF and reducing weld neck. All five share the same tapered hub and the same butt-weld end. What changes is the sealing face, the length of the barrel, or which pipe size the bore is cut for. Naming the sub-type in your enquiry saves a round of questions.

Weld Neck Raised Face – WNRF

The one most people mean when they say weld neck. Tapered hub, butt-weld end, and a small raised ring that concentrates the bolt load onto the gasket. Order it by nominal bore, drilling table and pipe schedule.

Long Weld Neck – LWN

Instead of a short hub it has a long straight barrel, so the flange becomes the nozzle. Welded straight into a vessel shell, header or column rather than onto a run of pipe. Give us the barrel length as well as the bore.

Ring Type Joint – RTJ / RJ

The face carries a machined groove that seats a solid metal octagonal or oval ring in place of a compressed gasket. Specified where pressure and temperature would chew through a soft gasket. State the ring number if your drawing gives one.

Weld Neck Flat Face – WNFF

Same hub, no raised ring: the sealing surface is flat across the whole face. Used when the mating half is cast iron, where a raised face can concentrate enough load in one band to crack the casting. Common on pump and valve connections.

Reducing Weld Neck – RWN

Keeps the outside diameter and bolt pattern of the bigger line while the hub and bore are cut for the smaller pipe, so you change size at the joint without adding a reducer. Always send both sizes — flange NB and pipe NB.

Where Weld Neck Flanges Are Used — and Where We Send Them

Weld necks are specified wherever the joint has to survive pressure, heat or repeated cycling — refineries, petrochemical units, boiler and steam lines, chemical plants, LPG and cryogenic storage, and marine repair work. Anything that gets bolted up, run hot, shut down and bolted up again is weld neck territory, because the tapered hub carries the load into the pipe wall instead of dumping it at a fillet weld.

Refineries & Tank Farms

Crude, product and gas lines, pump and exchanger connections, manifold headers. RTJ weld necks where the duty is hot and high pressure; WNRF for the balance of the plant. Bore cut to the schedule on your line list.

Petrochemical & Fertiliser

Reactor and column nozzles, reboiler and condenser piping, catalyst transfer lines. Long weld necks are the usual pick for vessel nozzles because the barrel welds straight into the shell.

Boiler & Steam Lines

Main steam, feedwater, blowdown and turbine connections in thermal and captive power plants. Thermal cycling is exactly the fatigue case a weld neck is built for, which is why steam specs rarely allow slip-ons.

Chemical, Pharma & Dyes

Corrosive and solvent duty where the joint has to stay tight and the bore has to stay clean. This is where S.S. 304, 304L, 316 and 316L weld necks go instead of mild steel.

LPG, LNG & Storage

Bullet and sphere connections, loading arms, tanker filling lines and bottling plant headers. Class and material are chosen for the service temperature; tell us the design temperature and we will confirm what we can cut.

Shipyards & Marine Repair

Darukhana is a ship-repair belt, so a fair share of our weld necks walk out of the gate to yards a few lanes away — ballast, bilge, fuel transfer and deck service lines, often against a hand-marked sketch rather than a drawing.

What is a weld neck flange?

+

A weld neck flange has a long tapered hub that is butt-welded to the pipe end instead of being slipped over it. The taper carries the bolt load gradually into the pipe wall, so the joint copes with pressure, temperature swings and vibration better than any other flange type. I.S. Engineering Co. machines them in mild steel and stainless steel at Darukhana, Mumbai.

Which weld neck flange sub-types do you make?

+

Five. Weld neck raised face (WNRF) is the everyday version. Long weld neck (LWN) has an extended barrel and is used on vessel nozzles and heavy-wall lines. Ring type joint (RTJ, also written RJ) is grooved for a solid metal ring gasket on high-pressure service. Weld neck flat face (WNFF) is for bolting to cast iron equipment. Reducing weld neck carries the bolt pattern of the larger line with the bore machined for a smaller pipe.

What is the difference between RF, FF and RTJ facing?

+

RF (raised face) has a small raised ring that the gasket sits on and is the default for most piping. FF (flat face) has no raised ring and is used when the mating part is cast iron, which can crack under the concentrated load of a raised face. RTJ (ring type joint) has a machined groove that takes a solid metal octagonal or oval ring instead of a soft gasket, and is specified where pressure and temperature are too high for a compressed gasket.

What is the difference between a weld neck and a long weld neck flange?

+

A standard weld neck flange has a short tapered hub that butt-welds to a pipe of the same nominal bore. A long weld neck has a much longer straight barrel that acts as the nozzle itself, so it is welded directly into a vessel shell or header rather than onto a pipe. LWN is ordered by barrel length and bore, not by schedule alone.

What is a reducing weld neck flange?

+

A reducing weld neck flange keeps the bolt pattern of the larger line size while the hub and bore are machined for the smaller pipe. It lets you step a line down at the flange joint without a separate reducer. Give us both sizes, the flange NB and the pipe NB, along with the drilling table or pressure class.

What materials and grades are available for weld neck flanges?

+

Mild steel to IS:2062 covers the bulk of our work, and stainless steel is available in SS 304, 304L, 316 and 316L. Tell us the service fluid and temperature and we will confirm the grade against your enquiry. Mill test certificates for the parent material are passed on with the consignment on request.
